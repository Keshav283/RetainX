    90-Day Churn Risk Model (AUC = 0.738)

    Evaluated on a strictly held-out partition of 14,084 test customers (38,546 customer snapshots). The model demonstrates meaningful discrimination between active buyers who reorder and those who cross 90 days without purchasing.

    • Validation Strategy: Customer-Grouped Holdout (ensures snapshots from the same customer never cross partitions, preventing target leakage).
    • Random Guess Baseline: AUC = 0.500 (diagonal dashed line). Our model delivers +0.238 lift above random baseline.
    • Class Weighting: Trained with class_weight='balanced' to counterbalance extreme non-contractual single-order skew.

    NLP Portuguese Review Model (AUC = 0.974)

    Trained and validated on 37,123 real Brazilian Portuguese customer reviews. Reaches exceptional discriminative performance due to clear lexical signals between praise and complaints.

    • Feature Space: 5,000 TF-IDF unigram & bigram tokens (e.g. "rapida", "nao recomendo").
    • Holdout Set: 20% order-grouped holdout (7,417 test reviews).
    • F1-Score: 0.952 with 95.1% Precision and 95.2% Recall.

    Mathematical Attribution Formula (Per-Customer Explanation)

    Because all features are normalized with StandardScaler prior to logistic regression, each coefficient βi directly reflects relative statistical leverage on the log-odds of churning:

    Log-Odds(Churn) = β0 + ∑ βi × [ (xi - μi) / σi ]

    A positive coefficient (red) pushes the customer towards churn, while a negative coefficient (green) acts as an anchor preserving customer retention.

    College Viva & Evaluator Defense Masterclass

    Anticipated technical questions from mentors and external examiners with first-principles answers.

    Viva Defense
    Q1: What is the exact accuracy of your models, and why is ROC-AUC a better metric than raw accuracy for churn?

    First-Principles Answer:
    "Ma'am, for our NLP Portuguese Review Classifier, overall test accuracy is 93.2% with an ROC-AUC of 0.974 and an F1-Score of 0.952.

    However, for our 90-day e-commerce churn classifier, reporting raw accuracy would be academically misleading due to the severe class imbalance of non-contractual e-commerce: 99.48% of customer snapshots naturally cross the 90-day mark without a repeat order! A dummy model that blindly predicts 'churn' for everyone achieves 99.5% accuracy but zero practical utility. Therefore, we evaluate using ROC-AUC (0.738) and customer-grouped F1-score (0.776), which measures true discriminative capability across all classification thresholds."

    💡 Evaluator Impression Tip: Mentors love when students understand the difference between raw accuracy and threshold-independent metrics like ROC-AUC in imbalanced domains.
    Q2: How did you validate your models without data leakage?

    First-Principles Answer:
    "We prevented data leakage at two critical stages:

    1. Temporal Snapshot Integrity: When building historical customer snapshots, features are computed exclusively using orders placed on or before the snapshot date. Future orders are strictly reserved for labeling the outcome.
    2. Customer-Grouped Holdout Partitioning: In e-commerce, active customers generate multiple monthly snapshots over time. If you run a standard random train/test split, snapshot 1 of Customer A lands in Train while snapshot 2 lands in Test, allowing the model to memorize customer identity. We used GroupShuffleSplit grouped on customer_unique_id: all snapshots for a given customer remain strictly in Train or strictly in Test."
    Q3: Why is churn defined as 90-day inactivity instead of a cancellation flag?

    First-Principles Answer:
    "Unlike subscription services (such as SaaS or streaming subscriptions) where customers explicitly click 'Cancel Subscription', e-commerce marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart, Olist) operate under a non-contractual business model. Customers never cancel; they simply stop returning. In industry, the standard mathematical formulation is to establish an Inactivity Window. We chose 90 days: at 90 days without a purchase, a customer enters inactive status. For customers still within the 90 days, our classifier predicts the probability of crossing that threshold before a repeat order."

    Q4: Why are the reviews in Portuguese and how does your application process them?

    First-Principles Answer:
    "Olist is the largest Brazilian e-commerce marketplace, so all customer comments were submitted by real Brazilian consumers in Portuguese. Rather than discarding or translating the dataset offline, we built an authentic cross-lingual architecture:

    • Our Scikit-Learn NLP classifier performs TF-IDF feature extraction directly on Portuguese n-grams to score sentiment natively with 0.974 ROC-AUC.
    • When displaying executive recommendations or drafting service emails, our Google Gemini 3.5 Copilot translates the complaint into fluent English and maps the root cause to verified retention protocols."
    Q5: How does the GenAI component (Gemini 3.5 + RAG) connect with the ML models?

    First-Principles Answer:
    "Standard LLMs hallucinate generic, untargeted advice when asked to save a customer. In RetainX, we built a deterministic Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline:

    1. The statistical ML model calculates the 90-day churn score and extracts top feature risk drivers (e.g. Delivery Delay = +0.18, Negative Review = 1 star).
    2. The RAG Retriever in knowledge_base.py scans domain playbooks and retrieves verified intervention protocols (e.g. Logistics Service Recovery Protocol, High Freight Voucher Strategy).
    3. These retrieved protocols, along with customer metrics and the translated Portuguese complaint, are injected into the Gemini 3.5 Flash context window, producing actionable, policy-compliant retention briefings and email drafts."
